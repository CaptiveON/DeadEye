\begin{abstract}
Open-weight language models are increasingly deployed as decision-makers, and small models are promoted for this role on cost and latency grounds. It remains unclear how much decision quality depends on parameter count and how much on the way a model is converted into a policy. We introduce \deadeye{}, a benchmark and open harness for measuring this. \deadeye{} has six procedurally generated, seeded decision tasks with known oracles (a multi-armed bandit, a contextual bandit, loan approval with distribution shifts, gridworld navigation, tic-tac-toe and blackjack) and five conversion methods: free-form generation, exact likelihood scoring of legal actions, a linear probe on hidden states, LoRA behavior cloning, and a probe on raw task features that uses no language model. Returns are normalized so that a random policy scores 0 and the oracle scores 1, and common random numbers make every comparison paired. We evaluate within-family ladders from 70M to 72B parameters against seven pre-registered hypotheses. Under generation, normalized score changes by \result{[slope $b$ with 95\% CI]} per tenfold increase in parameters, with \result{[steeper / indistinguishable]} slopes on planning tasks. A \result{[X]}B model fine-tuned with LoRA matches a \result{[Y]}$\times$ larger zero-shot model on \result{[k]} of six tasks, and \result{[does / does not]} keep this advantage under distribution shift. Likelihood scoring removes format failures and closes \result{[Z]}\% of the gap between the smallest and largest models. \result{[No / Some]} model reaches the UCB1 reference on the bandit. Code, configurations and per-decision logs are released.
\end{abstract}
