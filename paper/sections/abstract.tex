\begin{abstract}
Small open-weight language models are promoted as decision-makers because they are cheap and fast. Yet the same weights can be prompted, scored, probed or fine-tuned into a policy, and how this choice interacts with scale has not been measured under controlled conditions. We introduce \deadeye{}, a benchmark and open harness for this measurement. It has six seeded decision tasks with known oracles (a multi-armed bandit, a contextual bandit, loan approval under distribution shift, gridworld navigation, tic-tac-toe and blackjack) and five conversion methods: free-form generation, exact likelihood scoring of legal actions, a linear probe on hidden states, LoRA behavior cloning, and a probe on raw task features that uses no language model. Returns are normalized so that a random policy scores 0 and the oracle 1, and common random numbers pair every comparison. We test seven pre-registered hypotheses on within-family ladders from 70M to 72B parameters. Under generation, normalized score changes by \result{[slope $b$, 95\% CI]} per tenfold increase in parameters. A \result{[X]}B model fine-tuned with LoRA matches a \result{[Y]}$\times$ larger zero-shot model on \result{[k]} of six tasks, \result{[but not / and also]} under shift, and likelihood scoring closes \result{[Z]}\% of the gap between the smallest and largest models. Code, configurations and per-decision logs are public.
\end{abstract}
