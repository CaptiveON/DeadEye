\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Language models as agents and decision-makers.}
SmartPlay \citep{wu2024smartplay} uses six games, one of them a two-armed bandit, to probe nine capabilities, and finds that models differ by capability rather than by a single agentic skill. BALROG \citep{paglieri2025balrog} grades difficulty from BabyAI to NetHack: models make progress on the easy environments, fail on the hardest, and are often hurt by visual input. LMAct \citep{ruoss2025lmact} puts up to 512 expert episodes in the context of frontier models on tasks that include tic-tac-toe and grid navigation; more demonstrations rarely bring them to expert level. AgentBench \citep{liu2024agentbench} evaluates 29 API and open models in eight environments and names weak long-term reasoning, decision-making and instruction following as the main obstacles. GTBench \citep{duan2024gtbench} finds that LLMs fail in complete-information deterministic games, the class that contains tic-tac-toe, and that chain- and tree-of-thought prompting do not always help. GameBench \citep{costarelli2024gamebench} shows that reasoning scaffolds change scores substantially, and TextArena \citep{guertler2025textarena} collects at least 57 competitive text games with online ratings. On the method side, \citet{huang2022zeroshot} showed that plans generated by language models are often not admissible actions, and that mapping them onto the admissible set buys executability at some cost in correctness; ReAct \citep{yao2023react} interleaves reasoning with acting, and Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion} keeps verbal reflections on past failures in memory. These benchmarks establish that difficulty depends on task structure, the premise of the task-dependent slope in H1. Their model sets are not controlled ladders, though, and each fixes one conversion method. The design nearest to ours is that of \citet{jiwatode2026spatial}, who cross Qwen3 model size with thinking mode and planning horizon on spatial games; we extend it to several families, five conversion methods and six decision structures.

\paragraph{Exploration and bandits.}
\citet{krishnamurthy2024explore} ran GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and Llama 2 on multi-armed bandits under many prompt designs, and only GPT-4 with chain-of-thought and an externally summarized history explored reliably. EVOLvE \citep{nie2025evolve} relates regret to model size on context-free and contextual bandits and shows that algorithm-guided inference and algorithm distillation let smaller models explore better than larger ones without such support. \citet{schmied2026greedy} trace the failures of 2B to 27B models to greediness, with up to 55\% of arms never tried, to a frequency bias that fades with scale, and to a knowing-doing gap: rationales were correct about 87\% of the time, yet the chosen action often did not follow from them. \citet{harris2026explore} test exploitation on its own and find that LLMs often fall short of simple linear regression even there. \citet{monea2025icrl} show that models from 500M to 70B parameters can learn from reward in context, and \citet{binz2023cognitive} found no directed exploration in GPT-3. Two results bear on how a policy for these tasks should be trained. Fine-tuning on interaction data from many tasks transfers information gathering to unseen ones \citep{tajwar2025curious}, and algorithm distillation teaches in-context reinforcement learning by cloning learning histories rather than expert actions \citep{laskin2023incontext}. We use UCB1 \citep{auer2002ucb} as a realizable reference and standard regret definitions \citep{lattimore2020bandit}. H6, that exploration is poor without scaffolding, is therefore largely established already. What we add is its dependence on scale within families, its behavior under conversion methods other than prompting, and a separation of exploration errors from exploitation errors.

\paragraph{Scale, emergence and small models.}
Cross-entropy loss follows power laws in non-embedding parameters \citep{kaplan2020scaling}, which motivates the log-linear form of H1. Compute-optimal training \citep{hoffmann2022chinchilla} complicates any reading of such a slope: the small rungs of modern ladders are trained far beyond compute-optimal token counts (SmolLM2-1.7B saw about 11T tokens \citep{allal2025smollm2}), so parameter count partly stands in for tokens per parameter. Emergent abilities \citep{wei2022emergent} are the main alternative to a smooth slope, and \citet{schaeffer2023mirage} attribute many of them to discontinuous metrics, one reason we fit slopes to continuous normalized returns rather than to success rates. Within one recipe, few-shot ability improves steadily with scale \citep{brown2020gpt3}, and the length of task a model can execute keeps growing with size after single-step accuracy has saturated \citep{sinha2026illusion}. The case for small models comes from three directions. A position paper argues that they suffice for most agentic calls \citep{belcak2025slm}. In FLOPs-matched comparisons, a small model given more test-time compute can beat one 14 times larger \citep{snell2025ttc}. And post-training let a 3B model beat a 72B one on Frozen Lake \citep{dilkes2025reinforced}, which answers the in-distribution half of H3 for one task. A general-capability score such as MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu} would be an alternative x-axis for H1; we keep parameter count because it is the quantity a practitioner chooses.

\paragraph{Converting a language model into a policy.}
Each of our conversion methods has its own literature. Scoring answer strings by probability is distorted by surface-form competition between paraphrases \citep{holtzman2021surface}. Presenting all options and scoring the option symbol works only for models that bind symbols to options well \citep{robinson2023mcsb}, option identifiers carry a token-level prior \citep{zheng2024selectors}, and few-shot predictions lean toward majority, recent and common labels \citep{zhao2021calibrate}. For instruction-tuned models, first-token probabilities often disagree with the answer the model writes \citep{wang2024myanswerc}, and the written answer can be the more robust of the two \citep{wang2024lookattext}. Meaning-preserving changes of prompt format move few-shot accuracy by up to 76 points \citep{sclar2024formatting}, and the serialization of tabular inputs matters for LLM classifiers \citep{hegselmann2023tabllm}. As a policy, likelihood scoring of legal actions underlies GLAM \citep{carta2023glam} and TWOSOME \citep{tan2024twosome}; the latter shows that long action strings are penalized unless scores are normalized for length. Our scoring follows the conventions of lm-evaluation-harness \citep{gao2023lmeval}, and larger models assign better-calibrated probabilities to their answers \citep{kadavath2022know}. This work motivates H4, but it was measured on question answering, not on sequential decisions across a size ladder.

Probing has a parallel history. Linear probes on frozen layers \citep{alain2016probes} show what is decodable, which is not the same as what the model uses, and they need control tasks to be interpreted \citep{belinkov2022probing}. Intermediate layers are often better features than the last \citep{skean2025layer}, and models often encode the correct answer while generating a wrong one \citep{orgad2025know}. That gap is the mechanism by which a probe could beat generation on our tasks, and it is why we pair the probe with a control trained on raw task features.

Behavior cloning \citep{pomerleau1988alvinn} compounds its errors once the learner leaves the expert's state distribution \citep{ross2011dagger}, and LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} and QLoRA \citep{dettmers2023qlora} make it affordable at every rung of a ladder. Whether a cloned policy generalizes is contested. Supervised fine-tuning memorizes the rules it was trained on while outcome-reward RL generalizes \citep{chu2025sft}, which supports the out-of-distribution half of H3. Fine-tuning on diverse tasks \citep{tajwar2025curious}, frozen language models with RL-trained adapters \citep{szot2024llarp} and LoRA fine-tuning on human choice data \citep{binz2025centaur} all generalize to some shifts, which cuts against it. LIMA \citep{zhou2023lima} and URIAL \citep{lin2024urial} suggest that alignment mostly changes style and format. That predicts the second clause of H2, that base and instruct checkpoints converge under probing and fine-tuning, but it also threatens the first: a base model with a good few-shot prompt may close the gap under prompting as well. Return-conditioned sequence models \citep{chen2021decision}, DPO \citep{rafailov2023dpo}, GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,deepseekai2025r1}, multi-turn agent RL \citep{wang2025ragen} and self-play on text games \citep{liu2026spiral} are further conversions that \deadeye{} v1 does not include.

\paragraph{Reasoning and output format.}
Chain-of-thought helps large models, while small ones produce fluent but illogical chains \citep{wei2022cot}, and sampling several chains and voting buys accuracy with compute \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}. Several recent results show that more reasoning can hurt. Deliberation lowers accuracy on tasks where it also hurts people, such as implicit statistical learning \citep{liu2025mindstep}. On controllable puzzles, thinking helps only at intermediate complexity \citep{shojaee2025illusion}; reasoning agents overthink \citep{cuadron2025overthinking}; longer reasoning can reduce accuracy \citep{gema2025inverse}; and models of about 3B parameters or fewer do not reliably benefit from long chains \citep{li2025smallstruggle}. Strict output formats can suppress reasoning \citep{tam2024speakfreely}, and instruction-following scores such as IFEval \citep{zhou2023ifeval} may predict which models fail to produce a parseable action. Every clause of H7 finds support somewhere in this work, but on different tasks and different models. Switching thinking on and off inside the same weights \citep{yang2025qwen3} permits a cleaner test, and latency-normalized efficiency has not been compared across sizes and task structures.

\paragraph{Quantization.}
At a fixed total bit budget, 4-bit weights are close to optimal on zero-shot language tasks \citep{dettmers2023kbit}, and GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq} and AWQ \citep{lin2024awq} quantize large models with little loss. Precision-aware scaling laws predict that the penalty for post-training quantization grows with the amount of pre-training data \citep{kumar2025precision}. Small modern models are the most over-trained, so this is the mechanism behind H5. Qwen3 degrades sharply only at very low bit widths \citep{zheng2025qwen3quant}, and for quantized reasoning models the cost depends on size and task difficulty \citep{liu2025quanthurts}. All of these studies measure perplexity, knowledge or mathematics; for H5 there is a mechanism but no direct evidence on multi-step decisions.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology.}
We follow \citet{agarwal2021precipice} in reporting interquartile means and bootstrap confidence intervals, and \citet{henderson2018matters} in fixing seeds and hyperparameter budgets across all rungs. \citet{biderman2024lessons} document how much language-model evaluations depend on prompts, normalization and the choice between scoring and generation; we therefore report both conversions side by side and release every decision. Our blackjack rules follow the Gymnasium convention \citep{towers2025gymnasium}, and the oracles use textbook constructions \citep{sutton2018rl}. The logs keep every action score and probe probability, which would support a calibration analysis \citep{guo2017calibration}, but calibration is not part of the pre-registered analysis.

\paragraph{Cognitive and behavioral studies of decisions.}
Studies in the style of experimental psychology ask how models decide, not only how well. GPT-3 outperformed people on a two-armed bandit but showed no directed exploration \citep{binz2023cognitive}. Centaur, a 70B model fine-tuned with low-rank adapters on choices from 160 experiments, predicts human behavior and generalizes to new cover stories \citep{binz2025centaur}. Even GPT-4 falls short of human-like rationality when it must form and keep beliefs \citep{fan2024rational}, language models show human-like risk and loss aversion \citep{jia2024decision}, and they assume that people are more rational than people are \citep{liu2025rational}. Our oracles are normative rather than human, but these findings suggest examining the direction of errors, for instance whether a model stands too early in blackjack, and not only their rate (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
