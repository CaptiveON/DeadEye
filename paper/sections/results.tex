\subsection{H1: scale under generation}
\label{sec:res:h1}

\paragraph{Prediction.} Under \texttt{prompt\_generate}, normalized score rises roughly linearly in the logarithm of parameter count in each of the four ladders, SmolLM2 from 135M to 1.7B, Qwen2.5 from 0.5B to 7B, Qwen3 from 0.6B to 8B and Gemma 3 from 270M to 4B, each with three or four rungs (H1a), and the slope is steeper on gridworld and tic-tac-toe than on loan approval (H1b). \subsection{H3: adaptation versus scale}
\label{sec:res:h3}

\paragraph{Prediction.} In distribution, the smallest rung of each ladder, fine-tuned with \texttt{lora\_sft}, matches or beats the largest rung of the same ladder under \texttt{prompt\_generate} on at least five of the seven tasks, in at least three of the four ladders (H3a). The pairs are SmolLM2 135M against 1.7B, Qwen2.5 0.5B against 7B, Qwen3 0.6B against 8B and Gemma 3 270M against 4B, so the zero-shot model is 12 to 15 times larger; only the small rung is fine-tuned, which keeps LoRA within its 4B limit. Under the covariate-shifted and sign-flipped loan variants, the small adapted model loses more relative to its in-distribution score than the large zero-shot model does (H3b). Those variants run in the task-structure block, where the pair is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with LoRA against Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with generation, about five times larger.

\subsection{H7: reasoning budget}
\label{sec:res:h7}

\paragraph{Prediction.} With a budget of 256 new tokens, chain-of-thought (Qwen2.5-Instruct at 1.5B, 3B and 7B) and thinking mode (Qwen3 at 1.7B, 4B and 8B, against thinking off with 16 tokens) improve gridworld and tic-tac-toe for the models of 4B and above, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B (H7a); they do not improve the models below 2B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen3-1.7B (H7b); and they lower latency-normalized score, \norm{} divided by latency per decision, at every size (H7c).
